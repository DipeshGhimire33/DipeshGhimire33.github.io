Featured project: early sepsis prediction on 1.5 million ICU records.

Sepsis prediction from ICU data

A machine learning pipeline that predicts sepsis from hourly clinical readings, built on the public PhysioNet/CinC Challenge 2019 dataset. It covers exploration, cleaning, feature design, six notebooks of modelling and comparison, and a Streamlit demo app. It is an educational project, not a medical tool.

Where I made the call

These are the decisions that mattered, in my words, with the reason for each. The red ones are where I disagreed with the AI.

  • I overrode the AI

    Missing values

    The AI suggested imputing the gaps. I chose to forward-fill within each patient instead.

    A patient's last measured value stays theirs until a newer one replaces it, rather than being pulled toward the population median. This was a judgment call, not tested against alternatives, and the project README lists it as a limitation. Median imputation appears only in the baseline models that cannot handle gaps.

  • I checked the AI

    Model choice

    The AI recommended a model. I tested it against four baselines on identical patient-level splits: logistic regression, random forest, histogram gradient boosting and a linear SVM.

    XGBoost scored highest on PR-AUC and ROC-AUC. Random forest scored slightly higher on F1 and recall, which is why I report every metric and don't pick one that flatters a single model.

  • Safeguard

    Splitting by patient, not by row

    70% of patients for training, 10% for validation, 20% for testing.

    A patient has many hourly rows. If rows from one patient land on both sides of the split, the model can recognise the patient and the score looks better than it is.

  • Safeguard

    Choosing the alert threshold

    I picked 0.80 using the validation set only, then applied it unchanged to the test set.

    Tuning the threshold on test data would quietly leak the answer into the result.

The result is modest, and I say so. For context, a model guessing at random would score about 0.019 PR-AUC on this test set, because only about 1.9% of observations are positive.

What it can't do

  • At the chosen threshold it catches about a quarter of sepsis observations (recall 0.249), and roughly 1 in 7 of its alerts is correct (precision 0.136).
  • It has not been validated on any other hospital or population.
  • The reference ranges behind my engineered features are heuristic, not clinical standards.
  • The strongest feature was time in the ICU. That describes how the model behaves, not what causes sepsis.
  • It is not for diagnosis or treatment decisions.
